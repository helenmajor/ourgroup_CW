Incremental Testing

Incremental testing supported stable feature integration

Incremental testing was applied during the development process. After each main feature or game level was completed, we tested it separately first, such as checking whether the interaction worked, whether the result page appeared correctly, and whether the progress could be saved. Then, we tested it again after connecting it with other modules in the learning journey.

For example, after adding the progress-saving function, we completed one level, exited the page, and re-entered it to check whether the completed result was still shown. When new content was added, such as school data, country selection rules, or CV tasks, we retested the related level to make sure the original interaction, layout, and saving logic were not broken.

Alpha Testing

Pilot testing validated playful engagement and revealed clarity issues

We conducted an alpha-version pilot test with three real users to evaluate whether EduMap Journey successfully communicates its core "Playful Experience" objective. The test focused on whether the RPG-style interface reduced the anxiety of postgraduate planning, whether the interaction flow was understandable, and whether the gamified features could motivate users to continue exploring application tasks.

The results were analysed using six UEQ dimensions: Attractiveness, Perspicuity, Efficiency, Dependability, Stimulation, and Novelty. The score range is from -3 to +3, where values above 1.5 are generally considered excellent. Overall, the results show that the system performed strongly across all dimensions, especially in emotional engagement and novelty.

Alpha Testing Result

UEQ Six-Dimension Analysis

The radar chart shows that Attractiveness achieved the highest score, 2.75 out of 3, indicating that users had a very positive first impression of the system. This suggests that the RPG-style visual design, fantasy map metaphor, and interactive quest structure effectively changed postgraduate planning from a stressful information task into a more enjoyable experience.

Novelty also scored highly at 2.60, showing that users perceived the system as different from traditional planning tools, information websites, or commercial application platforms. This supports our design decision to use game-based metaphors such as map exploration, alchemy crafting, and mission nodes instead of presenting postgraduate preparation as a static checklist.

Efficiency scored 2.20, which suggests that users could complete the main exploration tasks without feeling that the playful design created unnecessary friction. Dependability and Stimulation both received 2.10, indicating that users generally felt the system was reliable and motivating.

However, Perspicuity received the lowest score, 1.80. Although this is still above the excellent threshold, it reveals a clear direction for refinement. Some users were not immediately sure what certain abstract roles, tags, or temporary alert messages meant. This finding directly informed our next iteration: we replaced vague identity labels with clearer academic indicators, such as GPA and planning stage, and upgraded weak toast alerts into richer milestone modals with more actionable feedback.

Alpha Testing Result

Macro Quality Analysis

The bar chart compares three macro-level qualities: Attractiveness, Pragmatic Quality, and Hedonic Quality. Attractiveness remained the strongest dimension with a mean value of 2.75, confirming that the system's visual identity and RPG metaphor successfully created a strong first impression.

Hedonic Quality reached 2.35, mainly supported by the high Novelty score and the positive Stimulation score. This means the system was not only usable, but also emotionally engaging. In the context of our project, this is especially important because the main problem is not just that students lack information, but that they often feel overwhelmed, anxious, or unmotivated when facing postgraduate planning.

Pragmatic Quality scored 2.03, which is also positive but lower than Hedonic Quality. This suggests that the system already supports useful task completion, but its clarity and guidance can still be improved. The lower Perspicuity score explains this gap: users enjoyed the concept and could use the system, but some interaction feedback and task instructions needed to be more explicit.

Therefore, the macro-quality results validate the main design direction while also identifying a practical refinement path. EduMap Journey performs especially well as a playful and motivating experience, but the next version needs clearer feedback, more transparent task instructions, and stronger scaffolding for first-time users.

Heuristic Evaluation

Nielsen’s 10 Heuristic Evaluation

A heuristic evaluation was carried out by the team to systematically inspect the interface against Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics. The evaluation confirmed that the current design already addresses all ten heuristics through concrete interface decisions.

  1. Visibility of system status – A real-time progress bar displays game progress clearly, and node statuses are distinctly indicated through colour coding.
  2. Match between system and the real world – A Chinese-English bilingual system is implemented to suit users of different language backgrounds. Interaction methods are easy to operate, including familiar gestures such as drag, click, and scroll.
  3. User control and freedom – A “Go Back to Previous Question” function is provided, and a “Restart Game” function offers a clearly marked exit.
  4. Consistency and standards – The layout remains unified across all sections. Screen adaptation design is applied, ensuring a consistent experience on both web and mobile platforms.
  5. Error prevention – The submit button is deactivated until all operation requirements are met, preventing premature submission.
  6. Recognition rather than recall – Game progress is recorded and retained. When revisiting a previously completed node, the information from the last session is displayed again.
  7. Flexibility and efficiency of use – Basic interactions (drag, click, scroll) have been checked to be smooth and efficient.
  8. Aesthetic and minimalist design – The unified layout and screen adaptation across web and mobile platforms provide a clean, distraction-free interface.
  9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors – The state changes of the disabled button serve as a clear visual cue to prevent errors before they occur.
  10. Help and documentation – Help documentation, a knowledge guide, and operation instructions are all provided within the system.

UEQ-Informed Iteration

Iterative Refinement Based on UEQ Feedback

The alpha test revealed that the system's main strength was emotional engagement, while the main weakness was interaction clarity. Users liked the RPG-style design and felt motivated by the map-based journey, but some of them were confused by abstract identity labels and fleeting feedback messages.

Based on this finding, we made two major refinements. First, we improved identity clarity. In the earlier version, the system used abstract role labels that were playful but not always meaningful for academic planning. In the refined version, these labels were replaced or supported by clearer academic metrics, such as GPA, target stage, and planning status. This change uses progressive disclosure: users still experience the playful RPG identity, but they can also access concrete information when they need to make serious decisions.

Second, we improved feedback visibility. In the earlier version, some actions only triggered short toast alerts, which disappeared quickly and did not clearly explain what the user had achieved. In the refined version, important actions lead to richer milestone modals. These modals provide clearer confirmation, explain the meaning of progress, and guide users toward the next action.

These refinements directly address the lower Perspicuity score from the UEQ results. By making the system status more visible and the feedback more actionable, the next version improves both practical usability and hedonic experience. The design remains playful, but it becomes more understandable and supportive for first-time users.

AI-assisted Development

Technical Reflection on AI-assisted Development

We used AI tools such as Codex and ChatGPT as development assistants during prototyping and implementation. All design decisions, evaluation findings, and final refinements were manually reviewed by the team before being kept in the portfolio.

Example 1

Guardian Guide expansion for Year 2 / Year 3 levels

Prompt

Asked AI to extend the Guardian Guide level structure by reusing existing Vue components and drafting bilingual Year 2 / Year 3 learning content.

Outcome

  • Vue component reuse was much faster than starting again.
  • Bilingual task text was generated quickly for early drafts.
  • Some educational advice became too broad and generic.
  • Some gameplay-learning mappings did not match our intended journey logic.

Action

  • Refined prompts with exact student stage, learning goal, and existing component names.
  • Manually rewrote content that sounded like general study advice.
  • Checked each game action against the real planning task it was meant to teach.
  • Kept final judgement on difficulty, wording, and progression inside the team.

Example 2

Portfolio navigation changed from long scrolling to section pages

Prompt

Asked AI to restructure the portfolio into separate section-based pages with shared navigation and smoother transitions.

Outcome

  • Routing and repeated navigation markup were generated quickly.
  • The first version still felt like a long scrolling page internally.
  • AI did not fully understand that the interaction should feel page-based.
  • Some transition timing and spacing needed visual adjustment.

Action

  • Refined prompts several times with clearer intent: separate pages, active nav states, and no hidden scroll sections.
  • Manually checked each menu link, current-page state, and mobile drawer behaviour.
  • Adjusted animation timing, spacing, and header layout by eye.
  • Used human judgement to keep the portfolio readable instead of only technically separated.

Project Impact

Project Impact and Human-Centric Value

EduMap Journey matters because postgraduate planning is not only an information problem, but also an emotional and cognitive burden. Many students face fragmented information, unclear timelines, commercial agency pressure, and anxiety about whether their profile is good enough. Our system responds to this problem through a human-centric design approach.

First, the system helps democratise access to planning knowledge. Instead of requiring students to rely only on expensive agencies or scattered online posts, EduMap Journey provides a self-guided pathway where users can explore school information, application materials, deadlines, and cases at their own pace.

Well-being

Planning support should reduce emotional burden

Second, the system prioritises mental health and emotional engagement. By using RPG mechanics, fantasy metaphors, progress feedback, and rewards, the platform creates an emotional buffer between the student and the stressful application process. This does not remove the seriousness of postgraduate planning, but it makes the process feel more approachable and less intimidating.

Third, the system supports better guidance from teachers or advisors. The back-end dashboard allows teachers to understand students' progress, engagement, and redemption status. This means guidance can become more timely and evidence-based, rather than only relying on students to ask for help when they are already confused.

Ethics and AI use

Responsible AI support remained manually reviewed

Finally, the project reflects responsible use of AI. AI tools such as OpenAI Codex, Google Gemini, and ChatGPT were used for permitted support tasks: coding assistance, wording refinement, implementation review, and labelled illustrative images. They were not used to define the project motivation, research findings, core design logic, evaluation interpretation, or human-centric justification. This helps prevent hallucinated content, protects academic integrity, and ensures that the final design decisions are grounded in real user needs.
