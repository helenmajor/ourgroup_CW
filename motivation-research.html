Journey RPG

A gamified study-abroad planning system for XJTLU students preparing for overseas postgraduate applications.

This portfolio reframes the project as an evidence-based UX case study, showing how we translated a fragmented, high-pressure planning journey into a clearer experience built around progress, guidance, and motivation.

The Why

The Why: Choosing the Social Track

We chose the Social track because postgraduate planning can escalate into a mental well-being challenge within the university student community. Navigating complex application pathways can intensify anxiety and decision fatigue because of information overload, unclear sequencing, and rigid delivery methods. This information asymmetry may push students toward opaque or costly commercial agency advice, which weakens educational equity.

Our motivation is to democratize access to academic planning and prioritize student well-being. By transforming an overwhelming chore into an engaging, community-supported journey, we address a critical social need. Traditional textual guides often fail to sustain motivation; therefore, integrating Western fantasy RPG mechanics, such as "alchemy crafting" for CVs, can work as an emotional buffer. It shifts the narrative from stressful execution to playful exploration.

Furthermore, this system bridges a vital communication gap by providing students with self-guided clarity while equipping academic advisors with a centralized hub for tracking and timely support. Ultimately, our goal is to foster a healthier, more transparent, and highly interactive academic ecosystem.

Problem Context

Planning pressure and student pain points

For many XJTLU students, study-abroad preparation is not a single task. It is a sequence of research, comparison, document preparation, and deadline management activities that can feel difficult to structure.

The project focuses on a student experience shaped by information overload, unclear priorities, long planning horizons, and emotional uncertainty.

Our design opportunity was to transform this complex process into a guided pathway that still respects academic seriousness while making progress more visible and manageable.

Scattered information sources

Students switch between university websites, social media, notes, and peer advice with no unified planning structure.

Invisible progress

Long-term goals such as applications or language tests provide little short-term feedback, which reduces momentum.

High-stakes comparison work

Comparing destinations, courses, deadlines, and requirements creates cognitive load and decision fatigue.

Uneven support and confidence

Students may receive advice from many channels, but not always at the right time or in the right format for action.

Academic Gap Analysis

The Gap: Literature Matrix

Category Papers Examined What it did well (Pros) What it missed (Cons) Design Implication for Journey RPG
1. Definition & Foundation Deterding et al. (2011), Hamari et al. (2014) Established frameworks distinguishing game elements and showed that gamification can boost engagement. Lacked focus on long-term, high-stakes academic anxiety and gave limited attention to emotional buffering. Use RPG elements, not only points and badges, as a psychological relief mechanism for stressful planning tasks.
2. Personalized Gamification Gamification Preferences in Digital Education; Development of a Gamification Model for Personalized E-learning; Investigating the Implementation and Effects of Personalized Gamification in Education. Validated that one-size-fits-all gamification fails and linked specific mechanics to diverse user profiles. Overlooked the dynamic shift of user needs over time, such as Year 2 exploration versus Year 4 execution. Implement a dynamic, phase-based system, such as Year 2 Goal Radar and Year 4 CV Crafting Table flows.
3. Educational Effectiveness Gamification-Based Learning Method for Hijaiyah Letters; UPOU MOOCs gamification study; Gamification in Education: A Passing Trend or a Genuine Potential? Validated concrete learning outcomes and sustained motivation through digital gamefulness. Focused mainly on micro-level coursework rather than macro-level life planning and multi-year tracking. Translate micro-learning rewards, such as virtual coins, into macro-planning milestones and offline tangible benefits.
4. Exploration Metaphors CareerPooler (2025) Showed that strong visual metaphors, such as pools or maps, can reduce anxiety and support exploration. Focused on initial exploration and offered less support for the heavy execution phase, such as sorting CVs and meeting deadlines. Combine visual exploration, such as star mapping, with intensive execution mechanics, such as Alchemy Crafting and Tower Defense.

Product / Competitor Analysis

Commercial Products and Market Gap

1. Habitica

Link: https://habitica.com/static/home

What

Habitica is a task management application that transforms real-life tasks, such as studying, daily routines, and work, into an RPG-style experience, using game mechanics to motivate users to complete real-world tasks.

How it works

  1. Users create an account and customise a character
  2. Add tasks (Habits / Dailies / To-do)
  3. Complete tasks to earn experience points (XP) and coins
  4. Level up the character and unlock equipment or skills

Core Features

  • Task management system (Habits / Dailies / To-do)
  • RPG character progression system (levels, equipment)
  • Reward and punishment mechanism (completion vs non-completion)
  • Team-based tasks (Party / Quest)

Strengths

  1. Strong behavioural motivation

    Directly links real-world tasks with game rewards, improving execution

  2. Clear structure

    Tasks are well categorised, making long-term goals easier to manage

  3. Strong sense of immersion

    Character progression helps sustain user engagement

Limitations

  1. Lacks content guidance

    The system does not tell users what they should do; it only records tasks

  2. No decision support

    It cannot help users make complex choices, such as study-abroad planning

  3. Limited personalisation

    All users follow the same mechanism, with no differentiated pathways

Relevance to our project

Habitica solves the problem of execution, but not the problem of decision-making.

Our system = decision-making + execution

2. Classcraft

Link: http://hmhco.com/classcraft

What

Classcraft is an educational platform that turns the classroom into an RPG game environment, using game mechanics to improve student engagement while allowing teachers to monitor student performance.

How it works

  1. Teachers create courses and classes
  2. Students choose a role (warrior / mage / healer)
  3. Complete classroom tasks to gain XP
  4. Teachers manage student behaviour through the backend system

Core Features

  • RPG role system
  • Classroom tasks and reward mechanisms
  • Teacher dashboard (monitoring system)
  • Team collaboration mechanics

Strengths

  1. Complete gamified system

    Transforms the learning process into a structured game experience

  2. Strong teacher monitoring

    Allows teachers to track student behaviour and progress

  3. Improves classroom participation

    Increases interaction and learning motivation

Limitations

  1. Teacher-led structure

    Students cannot use it independently

  2. Lacks personalised pathways

    All students receive a similar experience

  3. Does not support complex decisions

    Designed for classroom tasks, not life-planning scenarios

Relevance to our project

Classcraft is a teacher-driven system.

Our system = student-led system + management side

3. Duolingo

Link: https://careers.duolingo.com/

What

Duolingo is an application that helps users learn languages through gamified learning mechanisms, and is one of the most successful gamified education products today.

How it works

  1. Choose a language course
  2. Complete short tasks (multiple choice, translation, etc.)
  3. Earn XP and maintain a streak
  4. Unlock new levels

Core Features

  • Bite-sized learning tasks
  • XP and level system
  • Streak mechanism
  • Immediate feedback

Strengths

  1. Very strong user engagement

    Uses streaks and rewards to keep users active

  2. Fits fragmented time use

    Each task is short and easy to complete

  3. Immediate feedback

    Improves learning efficiency

Limitations

  1. Does not support complex learning goals

    Mostly suitable for structured knowledge acquisition

  2. Limited real-world action transfer

    Learning does not necessarily lead to application

  3. Limited personalisation

    The learning path is relatively fixed

Relevance to our project

Duolingo is learning-driven.

Our system = decision-driven + action-driven

4. Unifrog

Link: https://www.unifrog.org/

What

Unifrog is a platform that helps students with university and career planning, and is widely used by schools to support university applications and career pathways.

How it works

  1. Schools provide student accounts
  2. Students fill in personal information
  3. Browse universities and programmes
  4. Track application progress through checklists

Core Features

  • University and programme database
  • Application progress management (checklist)
  • Personal planning tools
  • Teacher monitoring system

Strengths

  1. Systematic information structure

    Provides comprehensive study and career planning information

  2. Clear task management

    Helps students organise the application process

  3. Supports school administration

    Teachers can monitor student progress

Limitations

  1. Lacks gamification

    The user experience is relatively dry

  2. Low engagement

    Students may easily stop using it

  3. Lacks emotional and motivational design

    Does not reduce application anxiety

There is a common tension in study-abroad planning between maintaining professional credibility and creating an engaging experience.

Relevance to our project

Unifrog is professional but lacks experience design.

Our system = professionalism + gamified experience

Market Gap

Current gamification research and existing commercial products share four major limitations:

  1. They rely too heavily on generic game elements
  2. They do not adapt well to high-stakes, long-term decision-making scenarios such as study-abroad planning
  3. They overlook individual differences and changing motivation over time
  4. They fail to balance the professional seriousness required for educational planning with the engagement and enjoyment needed to sustain participation

As a result, existing systems cannot effectively support students through the full journey from Year 2 exploration to Year 3 execution in postgraduate study-abroad planning.
