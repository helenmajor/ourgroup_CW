Guidance Design

Personalized and Reality-Oriented Guidance Design Expand to view details

Our system is not designed as a traditional right-or-wrong quiz with fixed scoring mechanisms. Instead of relying on standardized testing logic, the game-based interaction combines students’ academic performance, personal background, interests, experiences, and uncertainties in postgraduate planning to generate more personalized analysis and guidance.

The system is mainly designed for:

  • second-year undergraduate students who are still uncertain about future postgraduate school or major choices;
  • third-year undergraduate students who need support in personal statement writing, application preparation, and postgraduate planning;
  • postgraduate counselling teachers who need a more centralized and interactive way to provide guidance, especially when students may not be able to access important application information in time.

From the perspective of the implemented game design, personalization is reflected through how the system continuously records and reuses users’ previous choices across different stages. For example, in the first Year 2 game, students build a personal profile including GPA level, internships, research experience, competitions, projects, language scores, and GRE status. These inputs are not used to directly label students as “good” or “bad”, but instead become contextual information for later analysis.

In the Year 2 action-point allocation game, the system combines the previously created student profile with how users distribute effort across GPA improvement, language preparation, projects, research, internships, and information gathering. Different personalized feedback is then generated according to the student’s background. For example, two students may both invest heavily in language preparation, but the system may produce different suggestions depending on whether one student already has a valid language score while the other does not.

The system’s answers are also intentionally non-absolute. In the Year 2 destination-matching game, students’ choices across multiple questions gradually accumulate preference tendencies toward different application pathways such as the UK, US, Europe, Singapore, Australia, Hong Kong, or joint international programmes. Although the system eventually provides a suggested direction, users can still manually select and record alternative target regions according to their own real-world considerations. This reflects that the game is not attempting to determine a single “correct answer”, but rather helping users understand which pathways are more aligned with their current preferences and conditions.

Similarly, the Year 2 school-tier selection game uses the previously matched destination results to support later university recommendations and categorization into Reach, Match, and Safety levels. The focus here is not rigid scoring, but helping students understand application risk, target balance, and realistic matching between their profile and university expectations.

For third-year students, the platform also provides scenario-based application preparation tasks related to application materials. These include activities such as application material combinations, CV refinement, PS narrative ordering, and recommendation-letter communication strategies. Some tasks contain clearer feedback to help students learn basic application-writing conventions, but the overall purpose is still not to produce an absolute score. Instead, the goal is to help users understand how different expression styles, preparation sequences, and communication strategies may influence the application process.

Therefore, although the platform adopts gamified interaction to improve engagement and accessibility, its core focus remains reality-oriented exploration and personalized guidance. The system does not reduce students to a fixed numerical score, nor does it replace students’ own judgement with a single standardized answer. All generated results are intended only as supportive references to help users further reflect on their backgrounds, goals, and more suitable postgraduate pathways.
